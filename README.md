# Managing your time

How to manage your time, taught by your AI assistant: where your time really goes, urgent versus
important, planning a week with only a phone, procrastination, focus, life admin, saying no and
resting. Built for real lives with commutes, shifts, care duties and power cuts.

## This is not medical or mental health advice

This course teaches time management techniques. If stress, sleep or mood are getting in the way of
your life, see a doctor or counsellor.

## Known gaps

- **Most of the research is on students and office workers in a few rich countries.** We found
  little good research on time management for shift workers, carers, people with long commutes or
  people living with power cuts. The lessons adapt general findings to those lives; that
  adaptation is our judgement, not tested.
- **Time audits.** Logging your time is a standard self-help exercise, but we found no study
  showing that a time audit on its own improves results.
- **Saying no.** We found no strong research on how to say no well. A popular finding that saying
  "I don't" beats "I can't" has a weak evidence base, so the course leaves it out. Lesson 2's
  phrases are practical suggestions, not research.
- **The urgent/important grid** has not been tested as a method; only the urgency bias it targets
  has (see `facts.md`).
- **The Pomodoro technique** has one small study behind it (`facts.md`); its classic 25-minute
  timing is not proven better than others.
- **Life admin.** No research found on the best way to track bills and renewals; lesson 3 uses
  ordinary calendar reminders.
