# Lesson 6: Rest, so you don't burn out
<!-- facts: Sleep and shift work; Burnout; Unproven productivity claims -->

## Check in

Ask which apps they silenced, and how the focus block went. Did fewer interruptions make a
difference?

## By the end, the learner can

- Explain why rest and sleep belong in a time plan, not after it.
- Name the three signs of burnout WHO describes, and when to get help.
- Protect sleep and rest around shift work, commutes and care duties.
- Review their week and keep the habits that work.

## Key ideas

**Rest is not wasted time.** Managing time well is linked to wellbeing (lesson 1), and a plan that
leaves no rest will break. Put sleep and breaks into your week as fixed blocks, like a shift.

**Sleep first.** Adults need at least the amount of sleep in `facts.md`. Long awake hours affect
you a lot like alcohol does (`facts.md`). Waking at 5 a.m. is not a secret to success; enough
sleep matters more (see the unproven claims in `facts.md`).

**Shift work.** Plan your sleep before you plan your free time. Protect it with a dark, quiet
space, and avoid heavy meals, alcohol and caffeine before sleeping. The NIOSH course linked in
`facts.md` has more.

**Small rests count.** Five minutes outside, a walk, a cup of tea with no phone. When you're short
of time, short and often is fine.

**Burnout.** WHO describes three signs of burnout from work stress: feeling drained, feeling
distant or cynical about your work, and feeling less effective (`facts.md`). Exhaustion from
caring for family is just as real, even though WHO doesn't call it burnout. If these signs last,
time management alone won't fix them. Talk to a doctor or counsellor, and look at the
managing-stress course in this project.

**Review weekly.** In your planning time, ask: what worked, what didn't, what will I change? Keep
what helps and drop what doesn't. That review is the whole course in one habit.

## Worked example

Sipho works nights at a security company and has a long commute. He kept filling his days off
with errands and was always exhausted. Now he puts sleep in his calendar first, keeps one day off
free of errands, and moves life admin to his commute. He still has busy weeks, but notices sooner
when he's running down.

## Exercise

Ask the learner when they rest in a normal week, and whether any of WHO's three signs sound
familiar. If they do, gently suggest talking to someone.
